# HRX App — Experiences + WebViews

The highest-intent journeys shouldn't feel like handoffs.

## Overview
The HRX app's Experiences tab was designed to be the fastest way for members to book hotels, dining, shows, and on-property experiences. The challenge was that every booking ultimately lived on the web. My role was to design a system where app and web worked as one continuous journey, preserving trust, context, and conversion across platforms.

2025 — Present  
Lead Product Designer  
Hard Rock International

## Problem
Booking experiences in the HRX app meant crossing between native app and web environments at the most critical moment. Without clear context, location relevance, or continuity, these transitions risked breaking trust and killing conversion. At the same time, the app was launching in beta with only a handful of properties live, creating tension between relevance today and global scale tomorrow.

This was not a UI problem.  
It was a journey continuity and conversion problem.

## Strategy
Design Experiences as a booking layer, not a feature.  
Surface relevant, location-specific actions early, guide users intentionally into high-conversion web flows, and make web content feel like a natural extension of the app. Build the system to support a limited beta while clearly signaling the breadth of the global Hard Rock ecosystem.

## Outcomes
The Experiences tab became a centralized entry point for booking across hotels, dining, shows, and on-property offerings. Location awareness, focused CTAs, and optimized WebViews reduced friction and preserved momentum through the highest-intent flows in the app.

## Impact
The system established a scalable foundation for app-to-web booking across properties, aligning product, design, and engineering around a single conversion strategy. Early launches with Hollywood and Tampa proved the model, creating a blueprint to onboard additional properties with confidence.

## Designing Experiences as a booking layer
The Experiences tab was structured around action, not browsing. Instead of listing content, it surfaced clear booking paths for hotels, dining, shows, spa, cabanas, promotions, and retail. Each CTA tile adapted to the selected property, ensuring users only saw options that were actually available.

## Location relevance drives conversion
A single-select location control allowed the entire Experiences tab to reconfigure around a chosen destination. When a location was selected, imagery, copy, and link targets updated to reflect that property. When no location was selected, experiences routed to broader, global entry points.

This balanced personalization with exploration while keeping the system predictable.

## Transparency without disappointment
Because the beta launched with only select properties live, hiding the rest of the ecosystem would have created confusion and distrust. Instead, non-live locations were surfaced in a "Coming Soon" state, signaling scale and future availability without allowing dead-end selection.

## Strategic bets that shaped the experience
• Relevance before breadth — Only show experiences a user can actually book for the selected location.  
• Booking over browsing — Design every interaction to move users toward a transaction.  
• WebViews without handoff anxiety — App and web needed to feel like one system, not a redirect.  
• Prove it, then scale it — Launch with a small set of properties to validate conversion before expanding globally.

## WebViews as part of the product
Once users entered a WebView, the goal shifted to focus and momentum. Headers, footers, and unnecessary navigation were removed. App-aware layouts preserved context, kept actions visible, and ensured users always knew how to return without losing their place.

Web content adapted to in-app constraints rather than forcing desktop patterns into a mobile shell.

## Driving conversion before the handoff
For hotel bookings, an intermediary screen allowed users to select dates, guests, and see pricing or comped indicators before opening the full booking engine. This surfaced value earlier, reinforced trust, and positioned the app as a planning tool rather than just a launcher.

## Designing within real constraints
Each property operated on different backends, pricing systems, and technical capabilities. Rather than waiting for full alignment, the experience was designed to represent the ideal state and validated with launch properties. Proving conversion impact created leverage to bring additional properties into the same model over time.

This required constant coordination across product, engineering, and property teams, with design acting as the connective tissue.

## Experience outcome
Guests experience web content as a natural extension of the HRX app. Context is preserved, navigation remains clear, and booking flows stay focused from first tap to confirmation. No dead ends. No lost momentum.

## My retrospective
This project reinforced that the most important design work often lives in transitions. Treating WebViews as first-class product surfaces, not technical necessities, made the difference between friction and flow. If revisiting this work, I would push even earlier alignment on backend consistency to accelerate global rollout. The next step is expanding this system across more properties and deeper member-specific personalization.
